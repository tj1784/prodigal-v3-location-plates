The same pig fields as the reference image, at the same cold hour before dawn, with the camera at the same spot turned a quarter to the left. The pig pen's low wall of piled field stones stands six metres off on the left, dark muck inside, and the dark bristly pigs show on the left, a cluster about ten metres off and another about fourteen metres off, coarse black-brown hair, mud on their legs. The bare field of rooted dust opens on the right at thirty-four metres, stark and hoof-marked, and the low wall of piled field stones runs on the right at thirty-four metres, uneven and unmortared. The sun is behind on the right, a grey dawn, so the light comes from that quarter and the shadows fall forward toward the left. The fire down to embers lies to the left, six metres off, a faint warm edge on the pen stones and a thin smoke. Deep shadow holds the inside of the pen and the lower third of the frame. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
