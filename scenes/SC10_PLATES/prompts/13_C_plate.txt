The same pig fields as the reference image, now at noon instead of before dawn, the camera moved from just south of the troughs to open ground just north of the lone carob, looking across its shade. The stark bare field of rooted dust, the three weathered timber troughs dark with dry curled carob husks, the low wall of piled field stones, the stone pen, the reed booth and the one carob stay where the reference places them. The carob stands three metres off on the right, a thick dark trunk and a low wide crown of dusty leaves. The noon shade lies four metres off on the left, a hard-edged pool of dark on the rooted dust under that crown. Thorn trees of the east scrub stand from about sixty-nine to seventy-eight metres, all on the left, hooked grey branches and dusty leaves. The one light is the noon sun ahead on the right, high, hard and pale through dry haze. It strikes the crown's right side, and deep shadow under the tree covers the left third of the frame, the one shade in the field. A dry wind lifts grit off the hoof-marked dust. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
