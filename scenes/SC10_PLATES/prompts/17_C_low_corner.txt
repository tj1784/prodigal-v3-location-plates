The same pig fields as the reference image, at the same hard pale noon and in the same light, with the lens a hand's breadth above the rooted dust at the edge of the noon shade. Cracked dust, grit and a few dry pods fill the foreground, half in the hard dark under the carob and half in the pale sun. The carob's thick trunk rises three metres off on the right, bark split and dust-filled, the low crown cutting the top of the frame. The noon shade spreads four metres off on the left. Far on the left the thorn trees of the east scrub stand about seventy metres off, small and grey. The noon sun is ahead on the right, high and hard: it rakes the dust and leaves deep shadow across the left third, the one shade in the field. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
