The same pig fields as the reference image, at the same cold hour before dawn and in the same light, with the lens a hand's breadth above the rooted dust, looking toward the troughs. Dry curled carob husks and cracked dung-marked dust fill the foreground, small flies over the nearest pods. In the centre, five metres off, the three hollowed timber troughs rise just above the lens, their worn rims dark against the grey glow. On the left the bare field runs thirty-five metres to the low piled-stone wall. On the right the thorn scrub is a low grey band at seventy-two metres, thorn trees in the centre and on the right beyond it. The before-dawn light is ahead, the view backlit, so the trough rims are dark shapes. The ember fire behind the camera lays a faint warm grazed light across the near dust. Deep shadow covers the ground under the troughs, more than a third of the frame. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
