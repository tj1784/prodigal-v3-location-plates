The same pig fields as the reference image, at the same hard pale noon, with the camera at the same spot turned a quarter to the left. The noon shade lies one metre off on the left, the hard dark pool under the carob. In the centre, five metres off, a heaped oval of dry carob pods sits on the rooted dust, curled brown husks about a metre and a half across, the same pods that fill the troughs. A dark bristly pig stands about six metres off on the left by the trough row, and another roots in the dust about eleven metres off in the centre, flies hanging over the warm hides and the split pods. Thorn trees of the east scrub stand from about seventy-three to eighty-five metres, all on the right, grey hooked branches in the haze. The sun is behind on the right, high and hard, so the heap and the near dust are lit from that side and deep shadow falls to the left under the shade, more than a third of the frame. Grit moves in the dry wind. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
