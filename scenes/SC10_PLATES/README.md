## The locked design

### The estate (morning only)

- A mud-brick wall about 2.5 m high and 0.8 m thick, sun-faded ochre plaster, on a dry plain.
- The gate, about 3.2 m, between square posts. The wooden leaf hangs on the west post and is swung open into the court.
- Inside: packed-earth court. The citizen's house is the long flat-roofed range to the west. Storerooms and byres are the matching range to the east. Small high slits, weathered timber lintels.
- Outside the north wall: a strip of morning shade, the road along the wall toward the city, and the narrow track north-east to the pig fields.
- Hard pale dry light. One sun. Deep shadow in the shade strip. Empty of people and of animals.

### The pig fields (every later spot)

- One bare field of rooted dust, about 110 by 64 m, inside a low wall of piled field stones.
- Three long hollowed timber troughs in a straight east-west row at the origin, dark with dry curled carob husks. Flies over the pods.
- An oval heap of the same pods just east of the row.
- The pen west of the troughs, low fieldstone, dark muck inside. A narrow gate in the south side, wooden leaf on the west post, swung open outward.
- The booth just south of the pen: rough poles, a reed-mat roof, stone footings.
- One fire ring of blackened stones at (-10.4, -3.6). Before dawn it is embers. At noon it is cold ash. At dusk it is a new fire. At night it is the one warm light, a metre from F at the booth and 78 m behind E in the scrub. G's low fire at the troughs is that same night, folded into F.
- One carob south of the troughs, thick dark trunk, low dusty crown. At noon its shade is the only pool of dark in the field.
- East boundary: thorn scrub 67–72 m from the troughs, hooked grey trees.
- The cart track comes up from the estate on the south-west.
- Dark bristly pigs. Before dawn and at night they are in the pen. By day they are at the troughs and rooting. At dusk they are spread east, one drifting toward the scrub. In the night scrub one is fast in the thorns at the labelled tree.
- The estate is about 600 m south-west. It is named on the track, and it is a separate plate.
