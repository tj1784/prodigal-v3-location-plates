The same court as the reference image, at the same hour and in the same light, the lens now a hand's breadth above the paving, moved to a point four metres east of the court centre and a metre and a half south of the axis, facing a little north of east. Worn limestone and the low table's olive-wood leg fill the foreground, the table three metres off on the LEFT. The blazing brazier stands seven metres ahead at the centre. Open paving runs eight metres off on the RIGHT, the open shrine of Tyche eight metres off on the RIGHT, the crowned effigy nine metres ahead at the centre, and the east wall eleven metres off on the RIGHT. The thin moon is behind the camera. The brazier ahead backlights the table leg, and the colonnade lamps burn behind on the right. Long shadows reach toward the lens across the stone, and the dark under the table and along the shrine step covers a third of the frame. Ash has settled in the paving joints. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
