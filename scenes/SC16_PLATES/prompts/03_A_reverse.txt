The same hillside at the same deep night as the reference image, the camera at the same spot turned half round, facing 240 degrees and looking uphill. The waist-high dry-stone wall lies two metres away on the RIGHT, rough unmortared fieldstone with worn coping stones, dry grass rooted between the lower courses and small limestone chips at the foot. Beyond the wall the bare hillside rises through low fieldstone terrace edges, ochre earth and dry grass toward a faint crest under the pale warm-grey night haze. The low fire is behind the camera, twelve metres away, and this view is lit from the front: a weak amber spill reaches the near-facing ridges of the wall, while the joints, the wall base and the rising ground fall into real deep shadow covering a third of the frame and more. The six clay lamps remain on the terraces of the other quarters, each small flame close to its own bowl. A few wisps of dust lift from the dry grass in the breeze off the lake, and the same narrow thread of smoke rises behind the camera. The low fire remains the one flame and the only light that carries across the terraces. Fallen olive leaves and scuffed earth lie in the dark foreground. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
