The same hillside at the same deep night as the reference image, the camera at the same spot turned a quarter to the left, facing 150 degrees and looking down the slope toward the lake. The waist-high dry-stone wall lies two metres away on the LEFT, running downhill, its rough unmortared fieldstone and worn coping continuous with the enclosure. Dry grass, fallen olive leaves and loose limestone lie at its base. The slope steps downward through low retaining edges of piled fieldstone, ochre earth and dry grass. The dark lake begins forty-two metres away on the RIGHT, beneath the faint pale warm-grey haze, and the far shore is a low darker line. The low fire lies beyond the right edge, twelve metres away, grazing the source-facing wall edges from the RIGHT and sending their shadows to the left. The six clay lamps stay beyond that same right edge, each small flame close to its bowl on the terraces behind the turn. The recesses of the wall and the descending ground stay in real deep shadow, a third of the frame and more. A little dust lifts from the dry terrace lips in the breeze off the lake. The low fire remains the one flame and the only light that carries across the terraces. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
