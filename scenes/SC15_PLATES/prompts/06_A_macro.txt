The same far field as the reference image, a close texture of the wooden yoke on the headland. The lens faces north, half a metre from the beam, and the worn wood fills the frame: a heavy yoke, the neck-groove polished smooth, rope rash along the grain, old cracks packed with field dust, the raw end pale where the wood has dried. One ox stands just ahead on the LEFT, about a metre off, a dark flank and a curve of horn at the left edge, breath faint in the cold air. The second ox stands just outside the left edge. The terrace wall lies ten metres ahead at the CENTRE, and the wider field thirty metres at the CENTRE. The road east lies 150 metres on the RIGHT, the hill track 172 metres on the RIGHT, and the hill pasture 200 metres on the RIGHT, all soft beyond the wood. The low moon is behind on the RIGHT, a cold graze along the raised grain, and the groove of the beam falls into deep contact shadow. The dust in the cracks is the dust of the ploughing. Muted sun-faded earth colours, fine film grain.
