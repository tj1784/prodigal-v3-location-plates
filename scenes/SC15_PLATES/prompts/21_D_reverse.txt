The same gate as the reference image, a half turn, facing east-northeast out past the wall. The dry-stone yard wall, about 1.7 metres high, lies four metres on the LEFT, and the stone bench four metres on the LEFT. The yard runs five metres on the LEFT. The calf pen lies ten metres on the LEFT, the old fig ten metres ahead at the CENTRE, and the ox byre eleven metres on the LEFT. The hill track to the pasture runs thirteen metres on the RIGHT, climbing into the haze. The low moon is to the RIGHT, a cold light on the fig and the outer face of the wall. The gate lamp is ahead on the LEFT, just outside this turn, its pool about four metres across on the road behind the left edge. The yard fire, the spit and the eaves torch are behind on the LEFT, so a faint warm spill sits on the left edge while the fig, the track and the open hillside fall into deep shadow, more than a third of the frame. The night air is still. A little dust hangs along the wall, settled out of the yard. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
