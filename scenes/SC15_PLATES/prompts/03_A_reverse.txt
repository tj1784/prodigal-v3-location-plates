The same far field as the reference image, a half turn, facing southwest across the ploughing. The ground ploughed today lies seven metres on the RIGHT. The last furrow, with the wooden plough and its iron share standing in it, lies twelve metres on the RIGHT. The wider field stretches twenty-six metres on the LEFT. The yoke stays behind on the RIGHT, outside this turn. The terrace wall, the path and the road up to the farm lie behind the camera. The low moon is behind on the LEFT, so this view is lit from behind-left: the furrow ridges take a cold pale light on their near faces, and the shadow of each ridge falls forward into the next. The plough stands dark in its furrow. Deep shadow fills the near ground and the left of the field, more than a third of the frame. Fine dust still hangs in the furrows, lifted when the share turned the earth. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
