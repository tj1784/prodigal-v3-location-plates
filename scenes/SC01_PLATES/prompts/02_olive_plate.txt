The same hillside, wall, gate and olive as the reference image, at the same hour, now seen from uphill inside the wall: a terraced olive hillside above the Sea of Galilee, in the first century, in the late afternoon. The ancient olive's massive twisted grey trunk and broad low crown fill the left of the frame, twelve metres off, and at its foot lies the long, flat, knee-high shelf of natural pale limestone bedrock, worn smooth on top, a torn barley loaf and a few crumbs on it. Trodden earth, dry grass and loose stones run across the open ground; the waist-high dry-stone wall runs along the left edge, and on the right, thirty metres off, the wall opens at the gate of the reference image, its two square fieldstone piers and weathered braced leaf exactly as they are there, the olive grove beyond it. Past the olive, on the left, the pale lake and the grey hills of the far shore lie in haze. One low sun from the left breaks through the olive leaves in points; the trunk and the ledge sit in deep shade with bright rims, and long shadows cross the open ground to the right. Muted sun-faded earth colours, olive-grey leaves, a pale warm-grey sky, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
