The same walled olive hillside as the reference image, now at sunset in the first century AD. The camera stands six metres outside the gateway, facing as the reference does. The open gate sits six metres ahead at the CENTRE: two square fieldstone piers, a three-metre scuffed-earth threshold, and the timber leaf with its brace and rope lashings swung out from the uphill pier on the LEFT. The wall meets both piers. Its downhill run lies six metres ahead on the RIGHT, and the loose fieldstone sits on its top seven metres ahead on the RIGHT. The olive's long shadow lies eighteen metres ahead on the LEFT. Loose stones at thirty-two metres, the knee-high natural limestone ledge at thirty-three, and the ancient olive at thirty-six all lie ahead at the CENTRE. The pale lake and grey far hills lie thirty-nine metres ahead on the RIGHT. One low sunset sun stands ahead on the RIGHT, restrained and red through dusty haze, catching the wall top, the loose stone and the leaf. The dark faces of the pier and the wall throw long shadows toward the camera and to the left. Together with the grove shade, those shadows cover a third of the frame and more. A breeze off the lake lifts fine dust from the dry terrace lips. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
