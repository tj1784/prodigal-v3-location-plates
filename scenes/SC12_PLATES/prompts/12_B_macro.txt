The same piled fieldstone wall as the reference image, at the same harvest morning, as one worn stone filling the whole frame. The lens is close to the wall's north face at (4.0, 1.55), facing 260 degrees, so a single pale fieldstone occupies every edge. Its upper lip is rubbed smooth, the face pitted, with a hairline crack and harvest dust packed into the recesses. Lichen sits in the damp joints as small grey flecks. Beyond the crop the wall course continues 1 m ahead near the centre, with the ditch and the road just past it and thorn bushes from 17 m onward down the slope. One low morning sun stands ahead on the LEFT and grazes the raised grains. Tiny shadows fall into the crack and under the lip. Those recesses hold deep contact shadow across a third of the frame or more, against matte highlights on the worn ridge. Muted sun-faded earth colours, fine film grain.
