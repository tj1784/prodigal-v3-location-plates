The same piled fieldstone wall as the reference image, at the same harvest morning, as one loose weathered stone filling the whole frame. The lens is at the field gap's western pier at (11.6, 1.55), facing 255 degrees, so a single pale stone that sits proud of the course occupies every edge. Its top is rubbed, its sides chalky, with a seat of dust where it has shifted in the pile, and barley chaff caught in the crack beneath. Beyond the crop the gap and the wall lie 1 m away on the RIGHT, the ditch 1 m away on the LEFT, and the road 4 m away on the LEFT. One low morning sun stands to the LEFT and grazes the raised face. Tiny shadows fall into the crack and under the overhang. Those recesses hold deep contact shadow across a third of the frame or more, against matte highlights on the worn top. Muted sun-faded earth colours, fine film grain.
