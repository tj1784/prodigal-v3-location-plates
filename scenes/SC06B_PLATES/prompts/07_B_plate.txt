The same valley as the reference image, now in the morning instead of deep night, the camera moved from the high hillside above the farm to open ground at the bend. The stand is (1020, -592), on the south-west slope eighteen metres from the bend, facing 60 degrees. Morning in Judea, first century AD, 1.2 kilometres east of Eliab's gate. Dry grass and loose limestone fill the near slope. The road east to Jericho, four metres wide, rounds the bend eighteen metres ahead at the centre. The last spur of the hill rises twenty metres ahead at the centre. The boulders at the spur's foot rest twenty-three metres away on the RIGHT. The road continues east past the spur. Dust from the morning passage hangs in the ruts, and haze lies over the valley. One low morning sun, a little south of where it rose, stands ahead on the RIGHT. The spur's right faces and the boulder tops catch it, and long shadows fall toward the left. The shaded sides of the boulders and the dark grass in the near hollows cover more than a third of the frame. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
