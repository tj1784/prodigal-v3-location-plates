The same Jordan ford as the reference image, at the same noon and from the same dry west-bank stand, with the camera turned a quarter to the right to face two hundred and eighty degrees, downstream to the south. The thick west-bank tamarisk stands about three metres away on the RIGHT, its roots gripping the undercut earth and its branches over the near water. The brown current and the river margin lie about four metres away on the LEFT, the west bank on the LEFT beside them, the water running south between rooted banks. Fallen tamarisk leaves lie under the thicket. The high sun is ahead and the view is backlit: short shadows fall north toward the camera, and the thicket's dense shade covers the near water across a third of the frame. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
