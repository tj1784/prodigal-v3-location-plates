The same one-room farmhouse as the reference image, the stand point unchanged at the middle of the room and the hour changed from before dawn to dusk. The camera stands above the low heavy olive-wood table, facing north toward the hearth wall. The shell stays seven paces by four, 5.25 metres north to south and 3 metres across: mud brick, patchy lime plaster, sooted timber beams. The fire-blackened hearth ring and its clay pot sit 2 metres ahead in the centre. The arched lamp niche sits 3 metres ahead on the LEFT. Tall sealed storage jars stand 3 metres ahead on the LEFT, the oil jars 2 metres ahead on the LEFT. The clay water jar stands 2 metres off on the RIGHT, and the open timber doorway begins at the RIGHT edge, a bar of last grey dusk in the jamb. Beyond that edge the yard wall, 4 metres off, and the fold, 14 metres off, lie on the RIGHT. Bowls and flat bread share the near table with the one clay lamp, ahead on the right of the lens, the only light inside. Lamp smoke turns in that grey, and cooking smoke rises off the pot. Deep shadow covers the beams, the jar bases and the doorway, more than a third of the frame. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
