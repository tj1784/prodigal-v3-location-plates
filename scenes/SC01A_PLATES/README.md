## The locked design

One place, the foreign farm on the road west, first century AD, at hard white noon. The road under the wall, the top of the slope and the thorn scrub below it are all seen at this same white noon.

- The road is stony earth, 3.8 metres wide, running east-west.
- A shallow dry ditch, about 1.15 metres wide, lies on the road's north side.
- A waist-high wall of loosely piled fieldstone, about 0.9 metres thick, follows the ditch. A 3.5-metre gap breaks it east of the tamarisk. A 2.5-metre earth track climbs north through the gap.
- One tamarisk grows at the ditch, origin of the grid: a rough trunk and a low olive-grey crown.
- North of the wall, a last strip of green barley stands west of the gap, with pale cut stubble around it.
- South of the road the lip drops into a steep burnt scree-and-thorn slope, a sea of grey scrub lower down, and a dry wadi far below.
- About forty metres north, a low mud-brick farmhouse with fieldstone footings and a flat reed-and-earth roof sits in a small green fold on its own spring. The yard holds a few quiet hens. A well stands west of a long timber table under a vine. A rounded bread oven stands east of the table, a thin thread of smoke rising from it. The southern face has the main doorway, a small clay deity in the niche immediately east of it with an oil dish on the sill, and, farther east, the open pantry with its heavy timber bar.
- Worn: rubbed stone edges, wheel ruts, scuffed earth, split timber. A dry breeze lifts grit off the road margin. The sky is a pale warm-grey haze.
- The plates are empty of people. The yard keeps its hens, and only the views that reach the yard show them.
