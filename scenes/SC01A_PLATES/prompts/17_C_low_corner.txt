The same foreign farm and scree slope as the reference image, at the same hard white noon and in the same light, the lens a hand's breadth above the dusty gravel at the ragged southern lip, facing south down the fall. Pale grit and the chipped edge of the road fill the lower foreground. Dry thorn stems rise off the falling scree, a clump eight metres away on the LEFT and another fourteen metres away on the RIGHT, with further stems twenty metres ahead near the centre. The dense grey thorn belt begins about forty-two metres away at the centre, and the pale hazy distance sits beyond it. The same high southern sun is ahead, and the slope is backlit. Short shadows fall northward, toward the lens. The undersides of the loose stones and the thorn interiors stay deeply dark, covering a third or more of the frame, while the raised grit stays pale and matte. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
