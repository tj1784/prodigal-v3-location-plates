The same foreign farm and scree slope as the reference image, at the same hard white noon, the camera now about seven metres east and thirty-eight metres south, down on the open scree just above the scrub, turned to face due south into the thorn hollow. Loose pale scree fills the lower foreground. The dense grey thorn belt begins four metres ahead at the centre. A low grey clump stands six metres away on the LEFT and another six metres away on the RIGHT, their branches overlapping a further clump seven metres ahead near the centre. The shallow dust of the hollow lies about seven metres ahead near the centre, sheltered under those branches, with more overlapping clumps nine to thirteen metres farther in and a narrow opening continuing south through them. The dry wadi lies about forty-five metres farther down at the centre, burnt hills beyond it in the haze. The high southern sun is ahead, and the hollow is backlit. It catches the scree ridges and the upper thorn tips, and short shadows fall northward, toward the camera. The overlapping branches hold the dust and the stem interiors in deep shade across a third or more of the frame. Muted sun-faded earth colours, a pale warm-grey haze, fine film grain, wide lens, deep focus. The place is empty and still.
