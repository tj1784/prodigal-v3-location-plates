The same threshing floor as the reference image, at the same evening hour and in the same light, as one worn surface filling the frame: the shaft of the wooden winnowing fork where it lies on the north-east limestone rim. The camera is a hand's breadth from the wood on its east side, looking west-south-west along the shaft. Pale rubbed grain, old splinters and narrow dark cracks run through the frame, with crushed pale barley husks caught between the wood and the stone. The fork keeps its weathered brown timber, and the rim keeps its dusty chalk and ochre limestone. Just beyond this close frame, the husk drift lies one metre away on the LEFT, the low barley heap three metres ahead near the centre, and the hard threshing earth continues five metres ahead. The terrace edge remains ten metres away on the LEFT. One low sun stands ahead through the haze, and this close view is backlit, the light grazing the raised grain. The underside of the shaft and the husk-filled cracks hold deep shadow over a third of the texture. Muted sun-faded earth colours, fine film grain.
