## The locked design

- Terrace and yard. The farm sits on a terrace above the valley. The yard is 34 m east to west and 30 m north to south, inside a dry-stone wall about 1.7 m high and 0.7 m thick. The road runs east, then south-east, down to the village.
- The one gate. A 2.7 m opening between posts 0.9 m square, under a timber lintel. The olive-wood leaf hangs on the south post and stands open into the yard. From the road, facing west, that post and leaf are on the LEFT. The north post and the shoulder-high through-niche are on the RIGHT. The gate stays open.
- The lamp. A clay lamp in the niche. At night it is filled and trimmed. The west wind off the hills bends the flame out toward the road, and the flame holds, with a few moths in the glow. At first light the same lamp burns its last. The macro surface is that sill.
- Bench and trough. The bench runs 2.4 m along the outer wall north of the gate, at (0.65, 1.95). The trough stands outside, south of the south post, at (0.9, -5.6).
- The house. One room in the west wall, interior 7 by 4 paces, 5.25 m by 3.0 m. The timber doorway is at (-34, -1.8), on the gateway's axis, and stands open. From the middle of the room, facing the hearth wall: front, the hearth ring, the pot and the arched lamp niche. Right, the timber doorway and the water jar. Behind, the sleeping bench with sheepskins and blankets. Left, the storage jars, the oil jars and the hand mill on its reed mat. The olive-wood table and stools sit in the middle. At 42 m the wide plate reads the doorway as a small opening, the room plan held behind it.
- North of the lane. The fold, 14 by 10 m, barred gate facing south. The calf pen and the ox byre stand east of the fold. From the road the outer wall screens their lower courses. The byre roof rises above the wall.
- The fig at (6.5, 8). The floor track descends south beside the wall to the nine-metre threshing floor at (-14, -26.5), about 2 m below the gate. The wall screens the floor from these road views.
- Props that stay with the action. The papyrus stays on the knee, the reed in the hand, the wax with the steward until it is given, the signet with the writer. The design note's life line for the gate at night puts the dog at the father's feet. This scene's screenplay and the staging people list leave the dog out, so the empty plate does too. The fold holds the flock behind the wall. These prompts describe the wall and the fold, and the ground in frame stays empty.
